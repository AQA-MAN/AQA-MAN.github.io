1. Purpose

  • To describe the procedure for preparation, review, approval, issuance, withdrawal and archiving of SOPs.
  • To maintain a uniform pattern of format, content, initiation, review, approval, withdrawal and archiving of SOPs across all departments.
  • To define the accepted method of performing a particular activity in a correct and systematic manner.

2. Scope

This SOP is applicable to all departments of the organization.

3. Responsibility

QA Officer

  • Issues SOPs as the approved version.
  • Maintains the master copy and controls distribution among all concerned departments.
  • Ensures proper documentation as per the relevant SOP.
  • Maintains the master index and distributes controlled copies to concerned departments.

Department Executive / Chemist / Initiator

  • Prepares the SOP.
  • Ensures technical accuracy of process, materials, and conditions described.
  • Ensures availability/display of the revised SOP at the concerned working place, including translation into the local language where applicable.
  • Provides training on the SOP.

Head of Concerned Department

  • Reviews the SOP.
  • Ensures the procedures mentioned in the SOP are followed.

Head of Quality Assurance

  • Approves the SOP for all departments.
  • Ensures implementation and compliance.

4. Materials and Equipment

As per the requirement of the particular SOP being prepared.

5. Procedure

5.1 Preparation of Draft SOP

  • Prepare a draft SOP for review by the concerned departments.
  • Write the title and text in clear, plain language so that every person performing the activity does so in the same, safe way.
  • Where the SOP is used by personnel who may not understand English, the procedural section is also prepared in the local/vernacular language.
  • Use directive language with step-wise instructions — "will" / "must", not "should" / "may" / "might".
  • Text follows a consistent formatting standard: language, page size, font, grammar tense, margins, and single-sided printing, defined by the site's document-control SOP.

A typical SOP header block carries: department name, subject/title, a unique alphanumeric SOP number (department code + serial number), revision number, the SOP it supersedes, effective date, and next review date. An illustrative department coding scheme:

  • Body text is divided into numbered sections (1.0 Purpose, 2.0 Scope, 3.0 Responsibility, and so on), further sub-divided as needed (1.1, 1.1.1, etc.).
  • The footer carries signature/date blocks for Prepared By, Checked By, and Approved By, plus a format number and page numbering (X of Y).
  • Once the draft is complete, it is watermarked "Draft Copy" and circulated to the relevant department head for checking and correction.

5.2 Checking and Correction of Draft Copy

  • Check the SOP for sequential logic, technical correctness, and availability of the materials it references.
  • If the SOP touches another department's function, discuss and secure their acceptance of the proposed draft.
  • The department head forwards the checked draft to the relevant head for review.
  • Quality Assurance reviews the draft for technical correctness, regulatory requirements, and cGMP compliance.

5.3 Finalisation of SOPs

  • The user department incorporates review comments and takes a final signed printout on white A4 paper, signed in blue ink, dated in a standard format.
  • All SOPs are approved by the Head of Quality Assurance.
  • The signed original and its soft copy are handed to Quality Assurance.
  • The signed original is stamped MASTER COPY (green) on each page and retained by Quality Assurance as custodian.
  • Once signed and approved, the draft copy is destroyed.

5.4 Distribution of Approved SOP as a Controlled Copy

  • Controlled copies are photocopied from the master copy — white paper for controlled copies, yellow paper for copies meant for display.
  • Each controlled copy is stamped CONTROLLED COPY (blue), signed and dated by the QA Officer.
  • Display copies are additionally stamped DISPLAY COPY (blue).
  • The number and destination of every distributed copy is logged, with acknowledgement from the receiving department.
  • No further photocopying is permitted from a controlled copy. A spoiled or additional copy is requested in writing, the spoiled copy retrieved, and a new copy issued and logged.

5.5 Review of SOPs

  • SOPs are working documents and are reviewed whenever a working practice changes.
  • Every SOP is reviewed at least once within 24 months of its effective date, or earlier if required (including for cGMP non-compliance).
  • Maximum permissible review window: ±1 month of the due review date.
  • The initiating department updates the SOP in consultation with the relevant department head and forwards it to Quality Assurance.
  • A change control is raised for the revision — by the initiating department if single-department, or by QA if the SOP spans multiple departments.

5.6 Withdrawal and Archiving of Superseded SOPs

  • Withdrawal is carried out by the QA Officer.
  • The master document is stamped OBSOLETE (red) prior to issue of the new version.
  • Controlled copies are retrieved from every department they were distributed to.
  • Retrieved copies are destroyed and the destruction logged.
  • Only the last two obsolete versions are preserved by QA as historical reference.

5.7 Index

A department-wise SOP index is maintained for each department.

6. Training

Trainer: Head of Quality Assurance  ·  Trainees: all departmental/sectional heads  ·  Duration: one day.

7. Typical Distribution Pattern

  • Master copy — Quality Assurance
  • Controlled copies — Quality Assurance, Production, Quality Control, Quality Control Microbiology, Maintenance, Stores, Personnel & Administration

8. Annexures (typical set)

  • Format of SOP
  • Format register / list of formats
  • SOP issuance, distribution, retrieval and destruction log
  • Schematic diagram for SOP preparation
  • SOP requisition note
  • Department-wise SOP index
